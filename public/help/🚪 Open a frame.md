A frame is a folder, so you can go **inside** it. The canvas then shows nothing but what that frame
holds — no box, no border, no tab, and none of the rest of the map. Everything you do happens in
there: new cards belong to the frame, search and the outline list only its contents, and `F` frames it
rather than the whole map.

**Going in:** double-click a frame's empty interior, press `↑` with it selected, or right-click it and
pick **Open frame**.

**Coming back out:** **double-click the empty canvas** — the same gesture that took you in, since the
canvas is now that frame's inside. Or use the trail at the top-left — `My map › Research › Interviews`
— which is where you are: click any step to go back to it, and you land looking at exactly the view you
left. `↓` steps out one level too, and so does right-clicking the canvas.

Frames inside frames work the same way, so the trail can go several deep.

A few things worth knowing:

- **The canvas takes the frame's colour**, so you can see at a glance which one you're in. A frame you
  haven't coloured leaves the canvas as it was.
- **Its contents get the whole window.** A *horizontal* or *vertical* frame re-flows to the width you
  actually have, and folds back into its own box when you leave.
- **You can't drag a card out of the frame you're in** — there's nowhere outside it on screen. Leave
  first, or use "Move to…" in the outline view.
- **Leaving tidies up.** Anything you put outside the frame's old box grows the box to fit, so nothing
  you made in there gets hidden when the box comes back.
- **The link remembers.** Reload, or share the address, and you come back to the same frame.
- Reading a map you can't edit? Opening still works — it changes nothing.
